Up until now, we've focused mainly on the negative effects of a wandering mind. However, this mind-wandering mode - when we scatter our attention and focus - can also be immensely powerful.

This is the mode where our attention scatters to focus on nothing in particular

> Unline hyperfocus mode where you have to direct your attention outward, scatter focus is about directing it inward, inside your own mind.

Daydreaming has a bad rap, and part of this bad reputation is truly warranted: when our intention is to focus, daydreaming can greatly undermine our productivity. But daydreaming is a very potent tool when we want to solve problems, think more creatively, brainstorm new ideas, or recharge.

> As far as boosting our creativity is concerned, mind wandering has no match whatsoever

In scatterfocus mode, you leave the attentional space free around what you're doing at the moment - whether going for a run, biking, or doing anything that doesn't take up your full attentional space.

In terms of productivity and creativity scatterfocus gives you the ability to do three powerful things at once.

1. It enables you to set intentions and plan for the future.  Taking a step back and directing your attention inward helps you switch off autopilot and think of what to do next.
2. It also lets you recharge. Focusing on tasks all day consumes a good deal of mental energy. Scatterfocus replenishes that supply so you can focus for longer
3. Scatterfocus nurtures creativity. This mode helps you connect old ideas and create new ones. It lets you piece together solutions to problems.

### Use any 3 styles of scatterfocus to deliberately direct your attention inward

#### Capture mode This mode involves letting your mind roam freely and capturing whatever comes up. It's as simple as sitting down and sipping your morning coffee, with a pen and a notebook close by. Simply let your mind roam and be aware of where it wanders to. Write down all the ideas and plans that rise to the surface of your consciousness

> The capture mode is best for identifying what's on your mind

#### Problem crunching mode To enter this mode, hold a problem in your mind and let your thoughts wander around it, turn it over, and explore it from different angles. Whenever you get stuck on you catch yourself thinking about something unrelated, gently nudge back your attention.

> It is most useful when you're brainstorming solution to a specific problem

It enables you to solve complicated problems more creatively by providing nonlinear solutions you wouldn't necessarily arrive at while logically brainstorming with a pen and a sheet of paper.

#### Habitual mode This is the most powerful style of scatterfocus. The habitual mode is fairly easy: you simply do something habitual that doesn't consume your complete attention. This gives your mind space to wander and connect ideas.

To practice habitual scatterfocus, pick something simple that you enjoy doing. Then keep doing that one thing, and nothing else, until the mind wanders. For instance, going for a walk will uncover greater insights and connect more ideas than listening to music or reading a book.

This style of scatterfocus is best for recharging and connecting the greatest number of ideas.

**Scatterfocus is a creative mode that enables you to connect ideas in novel, non-linear ways. Here, you simply let your mind wander freely and pay attention to everything that rises to the surface of your consciousness.**
