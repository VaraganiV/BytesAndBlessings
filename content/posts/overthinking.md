## What Is Overthinking?

When we talk about overthinking, we're not talking about having our basic needs met, like a place to sleep or where we'll find our next meal. Those fundamental questions merit concern and require thought - sometimes a lot of it. Major life decisions like whether to change careers or move across the country require dedicated thought. No second thoughts.

Overthinking means those times when we lavish mental energy on things that don’t deserve it. We might feel stuck reviewing something we’ve done in the past or imagining something that might happen in the future. We might spend twenty minutes leaping to imaginative and serious conclusions about the short email from the boss or why mom hasn’t returned our call.

*Overthinking may look like fretting about the small stuff, devoting disproportionate amounts of brain space to the relatively insignificant.*

Whether the concern elbowing into our thoughts is big or small, these thoughts are repetitive, unhealthy, and unhelpful. It is exhausting and makes us feel crappy. 

Studies showed that overthinking makes life harder, hurts our relationships, and may contribute to mental disorders like depression, severe anxiety, and alcohol abuse.

## Habits to Prevent Overthinking

## Shifting Mindset

“I am kind of person who \_\_\_\_\_\_” is a very powerful statement. No matter what goes in the blank. A shift in our sense of identity or shift in how we fill in this blank will cause a massive change in our behavior. Starting now, don’t describe yourself as a chronic overthinker. Don't call yourself that, not even in your own head.

A shifting mindset is the first step but it’s an important one.

“Improvement won't happen on its own, and it won’t happen overnight, but it will happen.”

## Analysis Paralysis

It is an inability to make a decision due to over-thinking a problem. An individual can have too much data. The result is endless wrangling over the upsides and downsides of each option, and an inability to pick one.

Some common signs

1. Postponing a decision in hope that a better option will present itself
2. Constantly reviewing the same information we’ve already gathered
3. Fearing we will make the wrong decision.

What’s the cause

1. Intellectual curiosity - Highly intelligent people may see whole landscapes of possibilities that others don’t see. These positive traits have unintended consequences
2. Information Overload: Gathering data and examining options are beneficial but at a certain point, that hunt for information not only has diminishing returns but becomes actively unhelpful.
3. Perfectionism

What are the Antidotes

1. Get moving - You need to act. even taking a baby step forward can shift your momentum and get you unstuck.
2. Kick perfectionism to the curb

## Choosing Between Options

When making a decision between two good choices deciding on two good options sounds like a great position to be in, but it is surprisingly tough because there may be no “right” answer and no clear way to decide. Our instinct may slow down until the right answer becomes obvious but we need to be careful, we can plunge into analysis paralysis. Be careful ….

## Decision Fatigue

Each decision we make throughout the day takes a toll on our finite amount of mental energy. What to have for breakfast, which routes to take to work, handle the tricky conversation, etc. Each such small decision requires only a bit of brain power, but the cumulative effect is large.

“ The more choices we encounter the more likely we are to succumb to decision fatigue”

This is the state where we become exhausted from making decision after decision, our ability to choose breaks down. To avoid decision fatigue, it helps to think of our mental energy as would a budget - or more aptly a per diem.

“The more decisions we make in a day, the worse the quality of our decisions will be over time.”

Our mental capacity to tackle them erodes and we start to overthink Conversely the more decisions we eliminate the longer we‘ll retain our decision-making capacity throughout the day.

## When Things Are Beyond Our Control

An unexpected turn of events may throw us into momentary disarray, but if we can push through the messy middle of renegotiating our decisions in a hurry, we may find joy on the other side. The trick is knowing how to get there:

1. Make a choice - any choice is better than staying stuck debating our options, letting the moment pass us by.
2. Lean in, expect good things - Instead of striving to choose the ideal option, we can aim to choose a good one.
3. Build a margin for unexpected - One way to do this is by planning to meet deadlines early.

When we don’t recognize our overthinking behavior for what it is, it's’ impossible to get over it. But once we see what's really going on, we can begin to change.
