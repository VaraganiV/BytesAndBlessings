Our attention is overwhelmed like never before. Many of us are well aware that multitasking is intellectually tasking and inefficient. Despite this, we feel compelled to do so anyway while we fill each moment of our lives to the brim with mindless distraction.

Recent studies have shown that our brain has two powerful modes that can be unlocked When we use our attention effectively: Hyperfocus and Scatterfocus. Hyperfocus is a focused mode that is the foundation for being highly productive. Scatterfocus, on the other hand, is a creative mode that enables us to connect ideas in novel ways.

### Be conscious about the things you give your attention to:

On any given day, most of our actions and decision-making are done on autopilot mode. In fact, as many as 40% of our actions are habits, which do not require conscious deliberation. However, making some decisions on autopilot can be very detrimental. If we are not as conscious as possible while making these decisions, we find ourselves paying price even later years.

There are four categories of tasks that bid for our focus:

1. **Necessary work**  
   These include tasks that are unattractive yet productive. Team meetings and calls about your quarterly budget fall into this category. We usually have to force ourselves to do this work.
2. **Unnecessary work**  
   Here are the tasks that are unattractive and unproductive - like rearranging the papers on the desk. It does not lead to accomplishing anything.
3. **Distracting work**  
   This includes unproductive tasks that are attractive. Watching random youtube videos, chatting, etc.  
   The better you become at managing your attention, the less time you'll spend on tasks in this category.
4. **Purposeful work**  
   These are the tasks we're put on earth to do; these tasks we're most engaged in as we do them. the tasks with which we make the largest impact.

A perfectly productive person would focus mainly on **necessary** and **purposeful** tasks.

### Proper management of your attentional space

Our attention is the most powerful tool we have to live a good life and get stuff done, but our ability to focus is very confined. There is a finite limit to how many things we can focus on at once.

Out attentional space is what we're aware of at any given time. It is like a clipboard in our brain where we temporarily store information as it's being processed. When we decide to focus on something, that information occupies our short-term memory, and our attentional space ensures it's kept active so we can continue to work with it.

Given that this space is so small and can hold only a few things at once, it's vital we manage it properly.

Tasks take different amounts of attentional space depending on their complexity.

* **Habits**: things we can do without much thought and require minimal attentional space.
* **Complex tasks**: things that can be done well only with dedicated focus. these tasks occupy a large amount of attentional space.

The best way to avoid attention overload is to be more selective with what you permit into your attentional space. For instance, on the drive home, shut off the radio and process the day, and also plan tasks.

When your attentional space is overwhelmed, you feel overwhelmed. When your attentional space is clear, you will also feel clear.

### Filling your attentional space with a single, productive task puts you in hyperfocus mode

Contrary to popular belief, the fewer things you pay attention to, the more productive you will turn out to be. When you focus on a single task or project so it fills your attentional space completely, you put your brain in its most productive mode: hyperfocus.

Filling up your attentional space with a single project keeps you from being stressed and overwhelmed. your attentional space doesn't get cramped, and your work doesn't feel nearly as chaotic.

Choosing which tasks to work on ahead of time lets you focus on what's actually important at the moment.

The vital aspect of hyperfocus is that you're only allowing one productive or meaningful task to consume your attention space.

#### How do you enter hyperfocus mode?

1. Choose a productive or meaningful object of attention
2. Eliminate as many external and internal distractions as you can
3. Focus on that chosen object of attention
4. Continually draw your focus back to that one object of attention

Setting an intention for what you plan to focus on is the most important step to hyperfocus - the more productive and meaningful the task, the more productive and meaningful your actions become.

### Intention-setting helps you spend more of your day working purposely

Intention setting allows us to decide how we should spend our time; focusing our attention on that task gets it done efficiently.

Well, not all work tasks are created equal. Some tasks enable us to accomplish an incredible amount with every minute we spend on them.

#### Rule of 3 Choose 3 things you want to accomplish by end of the day. Note these are not your regular to-do list. These are the most important daily tasks.

#### Your most consequential tasks Make a list of all your tasks for the day and take a second to consider the consequences of carrying out each task - the sum of both its short-term and long-term consequences. The most important tasks on your list are the ones that lead to the greatest positive consequences. The hourly awareness chime One way to stay on track with your intentions is to frequently check what's occupying your attentional space to see if you've slipped into autopilot mode and reflect on what's important and consequential. To do this, set an hourly timer on your phone, smartwatch, or another device. When your hourly chime rings, ask yourself if: * Your mind wandering when the awareness chime sounded? * Are you working on autopilot or on some intentionally chosen to do? * Are you immersed in a productive task? By frequently checking in on your mind, you will be able to focus longer and spend more of your day working intentionally.
