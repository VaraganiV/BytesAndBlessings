Some trips are planned. Others are meant to be. This one — five days across Madhya Pradesh, ending at the feet of Mahakal on my birthday — turned out to be both. Here's every moment, exactly as it happened.

**5 Days · 3 Cities · 15+ Temples visited**

---

## Day 1 — The Journey Begins
**17th April · Thursday**

We started from Hyderabad on an afternoon flight, arriving in Indore by 5:00 PM. Checked into the hotel, and — honestly — did absolutely nothing that evening. Sometimes rest before a pilgrimage is its own kind of preparation.

---

## Day 2 — Omkareshwara: Where Two Rivers Meet
**18th April · Friday**

We woke at 4:30 AM and set off for Omkareshwara — a deliberate early start to beat the unforgiving sun and humidity. The early hour was worth every sleepy moment.

The darshan at Omkareshwara temple was deeply pleasant — calm, unhurried, and moving. After the darshan, we took a boat ride on the Narmada River, drifting at the sacred Sangam where the Narmada and Kaveri meet. The water felt impossibly still for such a holy confluence.

To complete the Omkareshwara Jyotirlinga experience, we then visited the Mamaleshwara temple — the second of the twin shrines that together constitute this Jyotirlinga. With both visited, the pilgrimage felt whole.

> "Being at the Sangam of Narmada and Kaveri — two great rivers meeting in silence — is not something you forget easily."

We returned to Indore by noon, rested, and then headed out for an evening visit to the famous Khajrana Ganesh temple. Dinner was at the legendary 56 Bhog Road — a street that lives up to every rumour about it.

*And yes — Phani spotted a "Louis Vuitton" T-shirt at a roadside stall and bought it for ₹900. Designer fashion, Indore style.*

---

## Day 3 — A Lazy, Delicious Day in Indore
**19th April · Saturday**

A self-declared lazy day. We visited Bada Ganesh and the Ganapati temple at a relaxed pace, wandered through the city, and spent the evening grazing through Chowk at Apna Sweets.

Breakfast was the highlight: Poha, Jalebi, and Kachori — the holy trinity of Indore mornings — impossibly good. Lunch was at Guru Krupa.

If you ever doubt why Indore consistently tops India's cleanest city rankings, one morning of eating your way through its streets will settle it.

---

## Day 4 — Ujjain: First Glimpse of the Sacred City
**20th April · Sunday**

We boarded the Vande Bharat, left our luggage at the MPT Ujjain hotel, and reached the city by 7 AM — fresh-eyed and ready. Ujjain has a way of announcing itself: the air is different here.

### Temples visited on Day 4

- Kal Bhairava temple
- Harsiddhi temple (Shakti Peetha) — Puja performed inside; Phani, true to form, was scared of the meditating devotees
- Kuja — same story; Phani inside for Puja, still spooked by silent meditators
- Sandipani Ashram — the ancient gurukul of Lord Krishna; meditated inside the Gufa (cave). Phani: terrified again.
- Mangalnath temple

After the temple circuit, we returned to the hotel, had lunch, and went for Mahakal darshan in the evening. Even the first visit carries weight — standing before Mahakaleshwar, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, feels like the city itself has been waiting for you.

---

## Day 5 — The Day That Will Stay Forever
**21st April · Monday — Birthday**

Birthday morning. Started the day with birthday wishes pouring in — and then rushed, as one rightfully should on a birthday in Ujjain, straight to Mahakaal.

> **Lesson learned the hard way:** We entered through Gate 1. Always use **Gate 4** — you walk significantly less and all the major temples are right there. Note it for your visit.

We visited Bada Ganapati and Harsiddhi Mata — one of the 18 Shakti Peethas in India. The evening aarti at Harsiddhi was something else entirely: the entire sanctum filled with the sound of Dhol and Nagada, the air thick with devotion. We stayed for the complete aarti. The best experience of the trip — perhaps one of the best of any trip.

> "Standing in the Harsiddhi courtyard as the Dhol and Nagada filled the night — this was the moment the whole trip had been building toward."

From there, we rushed to embrace Mahakaal. This time: a proper Abhishekam, offerings at multiple Shiva lingas, money donated — the works. Then to Ghat Kali temple for Kumkum archana — sitting before Amma, having her blessings fall on you, is quietly and completely fulfilling.

### Afternoon temple trail

- Kalabhairava temple
- Gopal Mandir
- Chintamani temple
- Chausati Yogini temple

By afternoon we were exhausted in the best way. Had lunch, slept. Evening: Mahakaal darshan again, this time on the free ticket line. Returned to the room and slept like someone who had just had their birthday blessed by an ancient city.

---

## Day 6 — Last Morning, Lingering Blessings
**22nd April · Tuesday**

The final day began the right way: Electral Water (pilgrim's best friend) and one last Mahakaal darshan. We also visited Harsiddhi Mata temple again — some places you return to without needing a reason.

*We forgot Bada Ganapati on the way out. He probably understood — we'd given everything we had to Ujjain. Next time, Bappa.*

Checked out at 2:00 PM. One last stop: Samosas and Kachori for the road. Boarded our flight back to Hyderabad at 6:00 PM, carrying something that doesn't fit in any bag.

---

## Overall — Blessed

To be in Ujjain. To stand before Omkareshwara. To have the Narmada beneath you and the Mahakal above. And to do all of this on your birthday — what more, truly, can one ask for?

**Blessed to celebrate a birthday in Ujjain.**
